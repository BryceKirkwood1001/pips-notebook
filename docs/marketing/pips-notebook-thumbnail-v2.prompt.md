## Full prompt

Use case: style-transfer / promotional cover edit.
Edit the supplied Pip's Notebook thumbnail into a genuinely restrained low-resolution 2D pixel-art indie game cover. Redraw the whole image with deliberate pixel clusters; do not merely apply a pixel filter to the existing illustration.

Preserve: the title exactly "PIP'S NOTEBOOK"; Pip as the recognizable little blue apprentice dragon with two short cream horns, purple eyes, pale belly and a tail; the warm wooden library setting and notebook-learning theme. Wide 16:9 landscape thumbnail with opaque background.

Art direction: take broad inspiration from the readable in-game sprite work, economical palettes and selective environmental detail of Stardew Valley and Moonlighter. Do not copy their characters, logos or compositions. Think an original 320x180 pixel canvas enlarged with crisp nearest-neighbor edges. A consistent chunky pixel grid across lettering, character and scenery; around 24-32 carefully reused colors. Two or three flat shadow tones per material, clean silhouettes, occasional small purposeful dithering clusters. Broad quiet areas between details. No subpixel edges, airbrush, smooth gradients, bloom, shiny bevels, faux canvas texture, particle glitter or excessive little highlights.

Composition: a cozy side-on library scene, closer to an actual game title screen than a glossy mascot poster. Put a large, flat, cream-colored custom pixel wordmark in the upper-left half in two lines: "PIP'S" then "NOTEBOOK", with a simple dark-plum one-pixel outline and small offset shadow. Give it breathing room and make it legible at small thumbnail size. No ornamental stars, gold metallic shading, swooshes, curls or fancy decorative serifs.

Pip: redraw as a modest-sized, expressive game sprite in the lower-right half, about one third of the image height, seated at a simple desk actually writing in an ordinary open paper notebook with a pencil. Simplify the face, smaller purple eyes using just a few pixels and at most one small white catchlight each; keep a slightly awkward, endearing curious expression. No giant glossy doll eyes or baby-dragon portrait. Show the hands clearly resting on the paper and desk. The notebook has a plain burgundy cover and cream pages, not an ornate magical tome. No glowing pages.

Environment: simplify the shelving into readable groups of books, a candle lantern above the desk, one quiet arched window with muted blue light, and a wooden floor. Warm ochre and dusty burgundy against deep ink-purple shadows, with Pip's blue as the focal accent. Light is indicated through flat color choices and small hard-edged patches, never cinematic lighting effects. Keep the scene slightly asymmetric and lived-in without clutter.

The only readable text is "PIP'S NOTEBOOK", spelled correctly. No subtitle, watermark, UI, buttons, extra characters, image borders, pseudo-writing or extra logos. The result should feel like carefully designed game pixels rather than a highly rendered fantasy illustration.

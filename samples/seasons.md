# Why Earth Has Seasons

Earth Science 101, Unit 4 handout. Read the three sections in order, then try the practice problems. Angles are in degrees and distances are in millions of kilometers (million km).

## 1. Axial tilt and sunlight angle

Earth's axis is tilted 23.5 degrees from the perpendicular to its orbital plane. The axis keeps pointing in the same direction in space all year, toward the star Polaris. Because the direction of the tilt stays fixed while Earth orbits, each hemisphere leans toward the Sun for part of the year and away from it for the rest.

Seasons are caused by the tilt of Earth's axis, not by Earth's distance from the Sun. In fact, Earth is closest to the Sun in early January, at about 147.1 million km, and farthest in early July, at about 152.1 million km. The Northern Hemisphere has winter when Earth is closest to the Sun.

The tilt matters because it changes the angle of sunlight. When the Sun is high in the sky, sunlight hits the ground steeply and its energy is concentrated on a small area. When the Sun is low in the sky, the same beam of sunlight is spread over a larger area, so each square meter receives less energy.

The noon Sun altitude is the angle of the Sun above the horizon at local noon. To find it, use this rule:

noon altitude = 90 - |latitude - declination|

The declination is the latitude where the Sun is directly overhead at noon. The declination is +23.5 on the June solstice, 0 on the equinoxes, and -23.5 on the December solstice. Northern latitudes are positive and southern latitudes are negative.

The intensity of sunlight on flat ground equals the full beam intensity multiplied by the sine of the Sun's altitude. At an altitude of 30 degrees the ground receives half of the full beam intensity, because sin 30 = 0.5.

### Worked example 1.1

Find the noon Sun altitude in Boston (latitude 42) on the June solstice.

Solution: The declination is +23.5. The difference between latitude and declination is 42 - 23.5 = 18.5. The noon altitude is 90 - 18.5 = 71.5 degrees.

### Worked example 1.2

Find the noon Sun altitude in Boston (latitude 42) on the December solstice.

Solution: The declination is -23.5. The difference is 42 - (-23.5) = 65.5. The noon altitude is 90 - 65.5 = 24.5 degrees. The Sun is much lower in December, so sunlight is spread out and weaker.

## 2. Day length

Day length is the number of hours between sunrise and sunset. Earth takes about 24 hours to spin once, and this spin rate does not change with the seasons. What changes is how much of each latitude circle is in sunlight.

On the equinoxes, day and night are each about 12 hours long everywhere on Earth. At the equator, day length stays close to 12 hours all year. Away from the equator, the hemisphere tilted toward the Sun has days longer than 12 hours and the hemisphere tilted away has days shorter than 12 hours.

The difference grows with latitude. On the June solstice, a city at latitude 40 gets about 15 hours of daylight, while a city at latitude 60 gets about 18.5 hours. North of the Arctic Circle, at latitude 66.5, the Sun does not set at all on the June solstice, giving 24 hours of daylight.

Long days add to the heating effect of steep sunlight. In summer a location gets more intense sunlight and gets it for more hours, so summer is warmer for two reasons.

The longest day of the year is not the hottest day. The ground and oceans keep warming for several weeks after the June solstice, so the hottest weather in the Northern Hemisphere usually comes in July or August. This delay is called seasonal lag.

### Worked example 2.1

A city at latitude 40 has 15 hours of daylight on the June solstice. How many hours of night does it have on that date, and how much longer is the day than the night?

Solution: A full day is 24 hours, so the night is 24 - 15 = 9 hours long. The day is 15 - 9 = 6 hours longer than the night.

## 3. Opposite hemispheres

The two hemispheres always have opposite seasons. When the Northern Hemisphere is tilted toward the Sun, the Southern Hemisphere is tilted away from it. The June solstice is the start of summer in the Northern Hemisphere and the start of winter in the Southern Hemisphere.

On the December solstice the situation is reversed. The Sun is directly overhead at latitude -23.5, the Tropic of Capricorn, and the Southern Hemisphere has its longest days. That is why December is a summer month in Australia, Argentina, and South Africa.

The dividing line is the equator, not a line between east and west. Two cities at the same latitude have the same season even if they are on opposite sides of the world. Tokyo and San Francisco are both in the Northern Hemisphere, so they have summer at the same time.

On the equinoxes neither hemisphere is tilted toward the Sun. The Sun is directly overhead at the equator, and both hemispheres receive the same amount of sunlight.

### Worked example 3.1

Find the noon Sun altitude in Sydney (latitude -34) on the December solstice.

Solution: The declination is -23.5. The difference is -34 - (-23.5) = -10.5, and its absolute value is 10.5. The noon altitude is 90 - 10.5 = 79.5 degrees. The Sun is high in the sky, so December is summer in Sydney.

## Common mistakes

> **Common mistake:** "It is summer when Earth is closer to the Sun." Wrong. Earth is closest to the Sun in early January, during Northern Hemisphere winter. The tilt causes the seasons.

> **Common mistake:** "Earth's axis tips back and forth during the year." Wrong. The axis keeps pointing in the same direction in space all year. Only Earth's position in its orbit changes.

> **Common mistake:** "Days are longer in summer because Earth spins more slowly." Wrong. Earth's spin rate does not change with the seasons.

> **Common mistake:** "The longest day of the year is the hottest day." Wrong. The hottest weather comes several weeks later because of seasonal lag.

> **Common mistake:** "It is summer everywhere on Earth at the same time." Wrong. The two hemispheres always have opposite seasons.

> **Common mistake:** "Seasons are opposite in the eastern and western halves of the world." Wrong. The dividing line is the equator, not a line between east and west.
